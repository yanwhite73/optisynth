# Robert Rich and Steve Roach — how repeated lines are made

## Robert Rich — performing the sequence and patching its repeats

For *Filaments*, Rich describes playing the running parts by hand, quantizing the performances, then sending the notes back out over MIDI to the modular. He explicitly traces that choice to Terry Riley. Quantizing fixes the onset grid while preserving which notes the player chose, where the figure turns around, where it leaves space and how long it takes to come back. An irregular phrase can sound as precise as a step sequencer. Rich later grouped *Neurogenesis*, *Filaments*, *Geometry* and *Electric Ladder* together for their shimmering patterned sound. Only the *Filaments* account specifies this recording method. [Rich in Innerviews](https://www.innerviews.org/inner/rich-1) · [Rich in Eighth Tower](https://robertrich.com/media/interviews/with-raffaele-pezzella-for-in-darkness-is-light/)

Two modular techniques from Rich's own patch notes explain different aspects of a bright repeated line. For his pitched percussion he sends a short trigger **into the audio input** of a filter just below self-oscillation. Cutoff selects the ringing pitch. Resonance changes how long the tone survives each strike. He layers takes at different pitches. For a delay patch he splits the sound near the end of the chain, passes one branch through an external delay and returns it to an earlier mixer, for example at the filter input. Each circulation passes through the filter again. A cutoff change can therefore colour a previous note while the next note is sounding. These are two independently documented techniques. [Rich's patch notes, question 15](https://robertrich.com/media/interviews/patch-tweak-april-2018/)

Rich also describes two quite different ways of generating motion. In his MOTM pulse-divider demonstration, clock division generates events. On the Prophet 12 he describes moving four delay times with slow LFOs or looping envelopes, using few repeats per delay, then adjusting overall feedback with filter resonance. A changing delay time can bend a tail between attacks. This is a way to put movement *inside* the returning sound. [MOTM demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z0qQYFA0dUg) · [Rich on Prophet 12 feedback](https://sequential.com/artists/robert-rich/)

## Neurogenesis — what survives at track level

Rich credits MOTM, the Synthesis Technology E520, Eurorack and several keyboard synths. The album notes assign tunings by track. They give no per-track patch diagram, delay interval, wet mix or clock source. Five12 appears in the acknowledgements. That alone cannot identify the sequencer for a particular part. [Rich's album notes and listening player](https://robertrich.com/album/neurogenesis/)

Start with the title piece and then the closer, *Erinacea*. In his listening account, Darren Bergstein singles out the running figure of the opener and the layered sequences and overtones of the closer. The album-wide tuning credits supply an explanation for why pitch matters even in a discussion of echo. A tap carries the previous pitch across the next attack. The two pitches form a temporary interval whose consonance, beating and colour depend on tuning and on the partials in each voice. [Album notes and Bergstein's review, reproduced by Rich](https://robertrich.com/album/neurogenesis/)

**An isolated listening experiment.** Alternate two short, harmonically rich notes, A at 220 Hz and a just seventh above at 385 Hz (7:4). Set one quiet repeat halfway between successive attacks and give the ring enough decay to overlap the next note. The seventh partial of A and the fourth of the upper note both fall at 1540 Hz. Change only the upper note to the equal-tempered minor seventh (392.0 Hz). Those partials are now about 28 Hz apart. Compare this first dry, then with the tap. This illustrates what tuning does to overlapping tails. The numbers and division are invented for the experiment.

## Steve Roach — playing the relationship between cycles

Roach describes changing the length of individual sequencers and combining them so the patterns continually shift against each other. His early setup paired three ARP sequencers with an ARP 2600. He also identifies the Roland Space Echo as important in that early studio. *Skeleton Keys* documents a later Synthesizers.com large-format system. Listen for where the high figure lands against the bass on successive passes. The bass can hold tempo while a different high note meets the same bass accent each time. [Roach in Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/interviews/161/steve-roach) · [Skeleton Keys](https://steveroach.com/discography/skeleton-keys/)

The transition from Part 3 to Part 4 of *The Desert Winds of Change* exposes the arrangement. John Diliberto describes the earlier sequences receding, a low delayed impact emerging, then synchronized patterns accumulating around it. Following only that low sound is a way to hear what the echo contributes while separate pitched events enter. Roach's session credits specify a live take without overdubs or post-production edits. For movements 2–5 he lists Moon and Doepfer sequencing and Eventide, Lexicon and Line 6 processing. The notes do not identify the particular delay on that bass part. [Recording, credits and Diliberto's listening notes](https://steveroach.com/discography/the-desert-winds-of-change/)

**An isolated listening experiment.** Put a low accent on step one of an eight-step lane. Run a seven-step upper lane against it on the same clock with no bar reset. On successive low accents the upper lane lands on steps 1, 2, 3 and so on. Now leave a hole after a bright upper note and send only that note to one quiet delay tap. Muting the send removes its answer. Muting the upper lane instead removes the moving accent relationship once its tail has died. Independent lane lengths, selective sends and independent mutes are the three controls that make this demonstrable. The lane lengths are illustrative.

## Rich's cloud oscillator

Synthesis Technology attributes the term to Rich's *Bestiary* sessions. He used 24 MOTM modules to make a swarm of sine waves. The E340 packages the idea as a bank of oscillators whose spread and independent pitch fluctuations are controllable. Hold one gate open and motion continues within that one sound. A test for a sequencer or delay is different: listen for a new attack or a returning copy of an earlier one. The *Neurogenesis* credits specifically name the E520 and leave the source of individual voices open. [Synthesis Technology E340](https://synthtech.com/eurorack/E340/) · [Rich demonstrating the Cloud Generator](https://sonicstate.com/news/2011/01/20/wnamm11-robert-rich-synthesis-technology/)

## A listening sequence for designing the instrument

**Roach, “Harmonia Mundi”.** Roach and Thomas Ronkin spent hours preparing patterns, then performed them live around 1982–83. One analogue master clock synchronized their sequencers. An interlocking line can change its relationship to another every pass while its attacks remain on an unwavering grid. Compare that setup with Part 4 of *The Desert Winds of Change*, where the delayed low sound gives the ear a reference while other patterns enter. For an Optisynth study, mute just the delay return, then restore it and mute one pitch lane. The two actions remove different musical information. [Roach's session notes and recording](https://steveroach.bandcamp.com/album/the-early-years-rarities-from-the-empetus-era) · [Later live recording](https://steveroach.com/discography/the-desert-winds-of-change/)

**Rich, “Neurogenesis” and “Erinacea”.** Follow one bright attack through its decay and into the following pitch. Try singing the successive *new* pitches first; then follow only the quieter returns. Do apparent answers preserve the same pitch contour and spacing? That is consistent with a delay. Do accented pitches change direction independently? A second part is another candidate. A stereo master can suggest both and cannot settle the routing. The opening and closing tracks use different tuning schemes. Rich identifies Riley as his strongest musical influence and describes harmonic relationships as places a pitch can settle even when the line itself bends. [Album player and tuning credits](https://robertrich.bandcamp.com/album/neurogenesis) · [Rich on tuning and phrasing](https://robertrich.com/media/interviews/15-questions-about-alternative-tuning/)

A patch study can isolate three things the recordings invite us to listen for. With one pitch pattern and delay division held fixed, move only resonator decay: successive strikes leave different amounts of ringing under the next note. Next hold decay fixed and slowly move only delay time: the returning pitch bends as the read position moves, if the delay supports continuous pitch-changing time modulation. Finally hold both fixed and change a second pitch lane's length: the accents realign even with static timbre. These are original experiments. Their controls suggest distinct design needs for the synth: local parameter movement, a delay with a defined response to time modulation, and independently cycling sequencer lanes. ## Separate reference for sustained chord performance

On the title piece of *Structures from Silence*, Roach captured his playing without quantization on an Oberheim DSX driving an OB-8. He kept the performance running and adjusted timing, filter and envelopes by ear. The studio had a Lexicon 224. A second OB-8 quietly doubled the first part well into the piece. A new section could arrive through a change in tone and width while the notes continued. [Roach on making the record](https://reverb.com/news/steve-roach-interview)
