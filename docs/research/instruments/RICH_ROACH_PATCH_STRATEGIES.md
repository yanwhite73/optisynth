# Robert Rich and Steve Roach — instruments and patch studies

Request 91. The artist accounts below identify equipment and working methods. The Optisynth settings are proposed listening experiments for 0.0.23.

## Roach — perform the breathing of a chord

On the title piece of *Structures from Silence*, Roach captured his playing without quantization on an Oberheim DSX driving an OB-8. He kept the performance running and adjusted timing, filter and envelopes by ear. The studio had a Lexicon 224. A second OB-8 quietly doubled the first part well into the piece. A new section could arrive through a change in tone and width while the notes continued. [Roach on making the record](https://reverb.com/news/steve-roach-interview)

Start with **String afterglow**. Turn both delay mixes down while shaping the voices. Try a 4 s amp attack, 8 s filter attack and 10 s amp release. Hold D–A–E, keep D and A sounding and move E to F. Listen for the new note gaining brightness while the old note is still fading. Record a second pass of the same notes, darker and an octave higher. Fade it up over a minute. Bring back the delay only as far as you can still hear the individual entrances.

## Roach — make the rhythm appear between two parts

*Skeleton Keys* was recorded with a Synthesizers.com large-format modular sequencer system. Roach describes combining patterns so their changing alignment creates another rhythm. His later live rig also included a Doepfer MAQ 16/3. These are examples from different sessions. [Album notes](https://steveroach.com/discography/skeleton-keys/) · [Tape Op interview](https://staging.tapeop.com/interviews/161/steve-roach) · [Live rig list](https://reverb.com/news/steve-roach-interview)

Open **Orbit / Four satellites**. Solo two rings together by muting the others. Give both the same step duration. Set the low ring to 3 hits in 8 steps, and the high ring to 2 in 7. Use D and A, full chance, zero drift. Their starts coincide every 56 steps. Rotate only the high ring by one step while playing. Record the transition. Add a quiet echo to the high voice once the two dry parts already suggest a third rhythm.

## Rich — separate the strike from the ringing body

Rich describes exciting a highly resonant filter with a short pulse and overdubbing differently pitched pings. Resonance sets how long the struck tone hangs. His other sources include homemade flutes, lap steel and mechanically excited sheet metal. In *Bestiary*, the MOTM modular shares the palette with granular processing, feedback and formant filters. [Rich on patching](https://robertrich.com/media/interviews/patch-tweak-april-2018/) · [Sheet-metal session](https://robertrich.com/media/interviews/conversation-with-jim-aikin-2001/) · [Bestiary notes](https://robertrich.com/album/bestiary/)

Start with **Glass loops**. Play three dry hits followed by a rest. Click **LFO** beside Decay on Modal Percussion. Try 0.08 Hz and depth 0.15. Listen for closely related attacks leaving different-length tails. For a more physical strike, play a short recording of a key hitting a cup through **Sample player OUT → Modal resonator IN**. Change Material while replaying the exact same strike. Then hold Material still and change the sample. This separates two decisions that a single percussion preset usually hides.

## Rich — constrain the pitches and let the tone wander

Rich describes working with overtone tunings, particularly seventh and sometimes eleventh harmonics. His patch examples include audio-rate filter FM and resonating noise. He singles out the MOTM-330 for PWM, the Synth-Tech 440 and Old Crow 480 filters, and the 4ms resonator and Intellijel Rainmaker for processing. [Tuning interview](https://robertrich.com/media/interviews/conversation-with-jim-aikin-2001/) · [Equipment and patches](https://robertrich.com/media/interviews/patch-tweak-april-2018/)

Start with **Just-intonation harmonic drone**. Hold a low D and its fifth. Play a high answering line sparingly, sliding into one sustained pitch with the bend wheel. For a second timbral study use **Reed squelch**. Play one repeated note and vary filter FM depth by hand against its already patched sub oscillator. Then vary the filter envelope amount while keeping FM fixed. Listen for the point where a pluck begins to sound like a small throat. For Rich's syncopated electronic work, his demonstration pairs a MOTM-650 arpeggiator with a MOTM-730 clock divider. Try changing the accent cycle against a steady run of notes. [Rich's clock-divider demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z0qQYFA0dUg)

## Put the two approaches in the same piece

Listen to *Persistence of Memory (For Dali)* and *The Grotto of Time Lost* on the artists' joint album *Strata*. Use the first as a reference for foreground detail and the second for sustained space. Over a slow pad, leave one short struck voice fairly dry and answer it with a single hand-played slide or breath sound. Mute each layer in turn. Listen for the struck voice defining distance and the played line giving the ear a phrase to follow. [Strata album page](https://robertrich.com/album/strata/)

A small physical setup for these studies would pair a polyphonic synth with two independent clocked patterns, an oscillator or sample player, a resonant filter or modal bank, a modulation attenuator, and a mixer with separate delay and reverb sends. The send levels let a player move just one sound into the distance while the others remain close.
