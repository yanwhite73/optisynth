# Playable space processors and the device programme

Requests 96–98. Start with a source-backed instrument that can turn short, tuned strikes into moving tails. Keep Movement at the design stage while adding destinations that can already be addressed from the rack matrix.

## First device — Prism tank

The selected reference is Matthias Puech's Miverb mode for Mutable Instruments Clouds Parasites. Its control surface and stereo tank make it a closer starting point for this task than another generic hall preset. The algorithm is an alternate firmware contribution, distinct from stock Clouds. Four input diffusers feed a pair of cross-coupled tanks. The pitch shifter reads the *feedback*, enabling successive rises or falls from one strike. Diffusion, size, damping, modulation speed and excursion do separate jobs. Freeze closes the input and recirculates stored sound. [Author's manual and revision history](https://mqtthiqs.github.io/parasites/clouds.html) · [Pinned processing source](https://github.com/mqtthiqs/parasites/blob/32fa66f5acce1bff2f0a7bdd041e29bad3222557/clouds/dsp/fx/oliverb.h)

Source review covers the repository README, Clouds manual and release history, Oliverb, the FX engine, random oscillator, coefficient and freeze mappings in granular_processor, and the raised cosine tables. The STM32 source headers carry MIT notices. Only the tank, delay-memory functions and random interpolation are adapted; hardware drivers, bootloader and sample-memory manager are outside the desktop dependency chain. The adapter processes local sample buffers and graph controls, has private persistent delay memory, opens no sockets or devices, and requires no external install. Removing the module releases its buffers when the previous graph retires. An unchecked read position or unstable feedback would affect the audio output; test extreme settings, freeze and tails under sanitizers. [Repository and licence](https://github.com/mqtthiqs/parasites/tree/32fa66f5acce1bff2f0a7bdd041e29bad3222557)

## What qualifies a flagship

We should be able to perform three passages in one patch: timed repeats maintain an identifiable motif; the repeats diffuse into a controllable pitched stereo tail; the player captures that field, plays a dry figure over it, then releases it on cue. Compare StarLab's tuned delay/string mode, Sealegs' delay character and Fog, and Cosmic Debris' Spray and Scatter over 16 interacting lines. Follow the sound when changing time and when muting input. Count a capability as delivered only after a repeatable audition and a usable panel exist. A claim of comparable depth also needs a player to perform the transitions in one take. [StarLab](https://www.strymon.net/product/starlab/) · [Sealegs](https://intellijel.com/shop/eurorack/sealegs/) · [Cosmic Debris](https://wmdevices.com/collections/all-modules/products/cosmic-debris)

The original design opportunity is the transition from a recognisable multi-tap rhythm into a diffuse tank while keeping independent control of dry attacks, tap positions, tank feed and held material. The current first device covers the tank. Tap capture, routing between individual repeats, physical string excitation and input/output matches against hardware remain separate work.

## Desktop adaptation

Prism tank retains the Miverb signal path, two-head windowed pitch shifting, interpolated tank taps and 16-bit delay storage. The 48 kHz adaptation scales line lengths, modulation travel, pitch windows and random slew from the original 32 kHz clock. The two damping filters are remapped in time. Raised-cosine windows use the vendored sine table. The random oscillators have private, deterministic seeds. Filter and modulation states are explicitly initialised. Regeneration extends to 1.18 for bounded self-excitation; Freeze uses unity feedback, opens the damping filters and stops new input entering the tank. The independent echo remains playable during Freeze.

The added input delay follows rack tempo or runs in milliseconds. Its feedback and audible return have separate controls. Set Echo return to zero to use it solely as the tank pre-delay. At zero wet mix, the stereo input passes directly through. The tank itself accepts the mono sum of both channels and generates a stereo field. Mix retains the original stereo dry image. The working memory includes a six-second echo buffer and one tank per reachable effect instance. Frozen audio is transient; preset recall restores configuration.

**Prism afterimage, revised for 0.0.25.** A short triangle voice sets the pitch. A velocity-shaped carrier at approximately three times that frequency supplies a brief FM attack, and a quieter modal strike changes colour between hits. A free-running LFO moves its brightness and blend. Play a repeating four-note cell with space after the second note. At the factory settings each dry event produces one quieter dotted-eighth answer. Feedback, tank return and pitch shift are zero. The Tank return parameter defaults to unity for older racks.

**Optical vapour, revised for 0.0.25.** Hold an open chord. The lower reed opens first; an upper octave enters over about 1.45 seconds. Pulse-width motion and optical-gate movement run at different speeds. Eight voices allow a common tone to continue when its neighbours change. The short pre-delay applies only to the quiet diffuse tank. Audible echo, echo feedback and pitch shift are zero.

The first versions in 0.0.24 put effects over existing factory voices. Yan rejected their timbre and their use of space. [Factory role guidance](../../project/SOUND_GOALS.md)
