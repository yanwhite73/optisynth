# Organic percussion in Optisynth

## Clay and water

The first kit plays at 92 BPM. Palm is a low bridged-T drum with attack FM. Wood rim is a struck bank of modes whose brightness follows the strength of each stroke. Water is an original synthesis voice with a short surface impact and a pitched ring. A separate fingertip recording slot feeds both a direct path and a tuned shell resonator. The factory contact is a short waveform generated by Optisynth. In Modular view, drop a WAV on the **contact** sample player, adjust its start, end and root note, then save a User preset. The current sampler references the external file by path and fingerprint. Moving the file requires relinking. Stereo WAV input is mixed down to mono.

Bubble entrapment is a documented source of the characteristic dripping-tap sound. In our musical model, size sets the ring frequency relative to the played note, decay sets the time for 60 dB of attenuation, and a short noise burst supplies the surface impact. The pitch-rise control is an expressive choice. Four strikes can ring over one another. Measured fluid simulation and microphone-space modelling are outside this first voice. [Phillips et al., 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-27913-0)

The two-bar bank A performance keeps the palm downbeat and principal rim strokes dependable. It gives quiet anticipations and water answers individual probabilities. Lane swing, velocities and pitches are editable. This follows a distinction in Compose's measurements of 465 Groove MIDI performances between recurring anchors, ghost strokes and lane-specific accents. The pattern was composed here. No performance transcription or commercial loop supplies its notes. [Compose groove measurements](../../../../optimystery-compose/research/groove_analysis_data.md)

## Recorded kit and pattern development

Compose already has an 18-sample frame-drum bank with 27 mapped regions from Versilian's CC0 library. It distinguishes large and small instruments, open and muted strokes, and hand hits. It is a practical source for a distributable recorded layer. Keep exact source paths, pinned revision, recording hashes and licence with the bank. [Compose bank catalogue](../../../../optimystery-compose/design/sounds/percussion-source.json) · [Versilian Community Sample Library](https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL)

For the next instrument iteration, use velocity to select a striking articulation and let repeated strokes alternate between recordings within that articulation. For local patterns, begin with one identified Wavedrum take and compare the original with its onset and velocity transcription by ear. Preserve inter-hand timing and accent relationships. A useful audition is the same two bars with fixed anchors, then with ghost strokes enabled, then with a locally substituted contact playing through the same shell. This separates groove, material and recording choice.

## Stroke suggestions on import

The Sample player analyses the original mono waveform once on preparation.
The **Suggested** line gives a possible musical role. Hover for the attack to
peak, active span, body character, and periodic pitch if one was resolved.
The engine measures energy below roughly 200 Hz, energy above roughly 2 kHz,
a short-time envelope, and periodicity during the body. It compares early and
late periodicity for modest pitch bends. On very long sounds or separated
repeated attacks it withholds a single-hit label. Continuous rolls, closely
spaced flams, heavy reverb and mixed loops can defeat those tests. The patch
keeps the audio and routing chosen by the musician. The classifier uses no
filename, uploads or training data.

Use the outputs as arranging suggestions. A low resonant stroke can anchor a
dum part, a bright short stroke can answer as tak. A rattling mid-body impact
can take a snare part. A closed hat or tick can articulate subdivisions, an open
hat can span one, and a softly swelling shaker stroke can mark the return
motion. The contact track in Clay and water is the replaceable top line.
These are choices for a player. In a dholak or pakhawaj collection, head,
damping, ringing body and pitch movement are more useful than automatically
assigning a named bol. Dhrupad is a musical tradition commonly accompanied by
pakhawaj. Its contrasting heads and stroke vocabulary merit a future test
set labelled by a performer. [Darbar on pakhawaj](https://darbar.org/the-pakhawaj-resonations-of-dhrupad/)

A local check used 17 named mono Hitsville one-shots to inspect the rules,
then 82 other variants from the same pack. Across those further variants,
all 14 hi-hats received a top-line suggestion, 30 of 31 toms a tom suggestion,
15 of 21 snares a snare suggestion and 10 of 16 kicks a bass suggestion. Six
kicks suggested low tom, and five snares suggested a tuned head. The labels
in the source distinguish instruments but do not establish open/closed hat
articulation. This measures same-pack consistency only. Shaker, tak and named
Indian strokes still need real labelled listening examples. Synthetic fixtures
also exercise damping, leading silence, level changes, silence and repeated
hits. No pack audio is included in the project.
